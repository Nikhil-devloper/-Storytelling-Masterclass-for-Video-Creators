s# Storytelling Formula

In the last lesson, we realized that the old formula:

> **Intro → Conflict → Resolution**

is more of a **plot structure** than an actual storytelling framework.

Now, let's discuss a new formula that I've been using for years.

And this formula works for **multiple types of videos**.

---

# The New Formula

> **Setup → Build → Change → Reflection**

This works for many different types of videos.

The most obvious example is **thought/idea-based videos**, where something changes in your mentality.

But it also works for less obvious types of videos.

For example:

* Product videos
* Travel videos
* Experience-based videos
* Documentary-style videos
* Even seemingly boring topics

A topic might sound dull on paper, but with the right storytelling, it can become interesting.

That's because **storytelling creates meaning around what happens.**

---

# 1. Setup

The setup introduces the world.

It answers questions like:

* Where am I?
* Why am I here?
* What am I doing?
* What is happening?
* Why should I care?

The setup gives the viewer the **context** they need.

But more importantly, it creates **curiosity**.

For example:

> "I wanted to find out what it's actually like to rent a friend in Japan."

Now the viewer immediately has questions:

> Why would you rent a friend?

> What happens when you do?

> Can a rented friend become a real friend?

Those questions pull the viewer forward.

---

# 2. Build

The build is where you show **how life was before the change**.

This is where you develop the situation.

You show:

* What happened
* What you experienced
* What you discovered
* The problems
* The small moments
* The things that made you question something

You're essentially building everything toward the eventual change.

Think of it as:

> **"This is what happened before I realized something."**

---

# 3. Change

This is the actual **movement of the story**.

Everything you've shown so far leads toward this moment.

You go through the experience...

You discover something...

And then:

> **"Oh... this is why."**

This is where the transformation happens.

It could be:

### Mental change

> "I thought X, but now I think Y."

### Experience change

> "I expected one thing, but experienced something completely different."

### Situational change

> "Things were one way at the beginning, and now they're different."

The important part is:

> **Something has changed.**

---

# 4. Reflection

Now we look at life **after the change**.

This is the resolution, but it's more than simply ending the story.

You ask:

* How was the experience?
* What did I learn?
* How do I feel now?
* What do I think about it?
* Did my perspective change?
* What does this mean to me?

This is where you give the experience **meaning**.

Think of it as:

> **"Now that I've experienced all of this, here's what I think."**

---

# The Formula in Simple Terms

You can think about the formula like this:

| Stage          | What it means                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Setup**      | Introduce the world            |
| **Build**      | Show life before the change    |
| **Change**     | Show the actual transformation |
| **Reflection** | Show life after the change     |

Or even simpler:

> **Setup → What happens → Something changes → What it means**

---

# The Extra Component: Hook

Over the years, I've added one more component to the formula.

### Hook → Setup → Build → Change → Reflection

The **hook comes at the beginning**.

Its job is to make the viewer want to continue watching.

The hook doesn't necessarily explain everything.

Instead, it creates a **question, curiosity, tension, or promise**.

For example:

> "I spent ₹10,000 to rent a friend in Japan. I wanted to know if a fake friendship could eventually become real."

Now I want to know what happened.

That's the purpose of the hook.

---

# The Complete Formula

> **Hook → Setup → Build → Change → Reflection**

### Hook

**Why should I keep watching?**

### Setup

**Where are we and why are we here?**

### Build

**What happens before the change?**

### Change

**What actually changes?**

### Reflection

**What does it mean after everything happened?**

---

# But Knowing the Formula Isn't Enough

Knowing the formula is useful.

But simply knowing:

> **Hook → Setup → Build → Change → Reflection**

doesn't automatically make you a good storyteller.

You need to understand **why each part exists**, how they connect, and how to use them without making the video feel like a formula.

Because the goal isn't to make the viewer think:

> "Oh, this is the Setup. Now we're entering the Build."

The goal is for the viewer to simply feel:

> **"I want to know what happens next."**

That deeper understanding is what allows you to use this formula to its **full potential**.
