# Storytelling Notes: Using “But” and “Therefore”

## 1. Core Concept

A common storytelling mistake is using “and, and, and” to connect events.

This makes a story sound like a list of events rather than an interesting sequence of experiences.

Key principle: Use words like “but” and “therefore” to create tension, suspense, and a logical flow between events.

## 2. The Problem with “And, And, And”

When every event is connected using “and”:

* The story becomes predictable and monotonous.

* Events feel disconnected or simply listed.

* There is little tension or suspense.

* The audience has less reason to stay engaged.

Example:

“I went to Japan, and I wanted a new experience, and I went to an internet cafe, and it was cramped, and it smelled bad, and I didn't have a good night.”

This describes what happened, but it lacks a compelling narrative flow.

## 3. The Solution: “But” and “Therefore”

BUT

Creates conflict, tension, or an obstacle.

Something happens that contradicts expectations or changes the situation.

Example: “I wanted to have an amazing experience, but I didn't have enough money for a hotel.”

THEREFORE

Creates cause and effect.

Something happens, so the character makes a decision or experiences a consequence.

Example: “I didn't have enough money for a hotel, therefore I decided to stay in a 24-hour internet cafe.”

These transitions make the story feel connected, dynamic, and engaging.

## 4. Example: Japan Internet Cafe Story

### Version 1 — Weak storytelling

“I went to Japan, and I wanted a new experience, and I went to a 24-hour internet cafe. It was cramped, and people could smoke, and it smelled bad, and I didn't have a good night.”

Problem: The events are simply listed using “and.”

### Version 2 — Improved storytelling

“I was really excited to go to Japan, but I didn't have that much money. Therefore, instead of staying in a hotel, I decided to visit a 24-hour internet cafe.

But the place was really cramped, and they allowed people to smoke cigarettes there. Therefore, the room smelled like cigarettes, which wasn't great.

How was my night?”

Why it works:

* “But” introduces unexpected problems.

* “Therefore” explains decisions and consequences.

* Each event creates a reason to keep listening.

* The audience experiences the story's ups and downs.

## 6. Applying This to Video Scripts

When writing scripts, look for opportunities to introduce contrast, obstacles, and consequences.

Examples from the lecture:

* “I genuinely want to spend more time, but my stomach has other plans.”

* “I thought people were romanticizing it, but I decided to try it anyway.”

* “I traveled Japan completely alone, but it wasn't the normal solo travel you usually see.”

These transitions make the narrative feel less predictable and encourage viewers to wonder what happens next.

## 7. Practical Exercise

When reviewing your next video script:

Script review checklist

0 of 5

Identify sentences connected mainly by “and.”

Replace some “and” transitions with “but” where there is a genuine contrast.

Use “therefore” or “so” to explain consequences and decisions.

Check whether each sentence creates curiosity or moves the story forward.

Read the script aloud and check that it sounds natural.

## Key Takeaway

Don't just tell the audience what happened. Show them what changed, what went wrong, and what happened because of it.

Use “but” to create tension and “therefore” to create cause and effect. These simple changes can make your storytelling and video scripts significantly more engaging.
