# What Is Story?

At school, they taught us:

> **Intro → Conflict → Resolution**

But this is more of a **plot structure** and not necessarily a story.

Let's take an example.

### A Simple Bus Story

* I missed the bus in the morning.
* I ran to the bus station, but it was simply too late.
* I was mad. I had an important meeting, and now I had to be late.
* I waited 15 minutes and caught the next bus.
* In the end, everything worked out.

This technically has a beginning, conflict, and resolution.

But it doesn't focus on **meaning**.

It's predictable.

And that's why it's so boring.

So, with that said...

# What Is Actually a Story?

> **Story = Change**

A story captures the **transformation that happens between the beginning and the end.**

Remember our last story:

> "I wanted to catch the bus."

It is a story, technically.

But it's a pretty boring story.

Now, if I had to tell you the same story in video format, maybe I would tell it like this:

> I was very angry at first.
> I'm always rushing from point A to point B.
>
> But while I waited, I just stood there doing nothing for once.
>
> Watching the cars go by, I realized I hadn't slowed down in months.
>
> The next bus came.
>
> And for the first time, I wasn't mad at all.

Now there is something different.

The **events** are almost the same.

But the **person has changed.**

### The Change

**Beginning:**

> "I'm always rushing."

**End:**

> "I realized I hadn't slowed down in months."

The change is in the person's **mentality**.

That's what makes it a story.

---

# If There Is No Change, You Don't Have a Story

Because **change gives meaning and direction to what happens.**

Compare the first bus story with the second one.

The first story is:

> Missed bus → Waited → Caught next bus → Everything worked out.

It's predictable.

The second story is:

> Missed bus → Forced to stop → Reflected → Realized something → Changed mentality.

The second one is less predictable because we actually **discover something**.

The change is:

> **My mentality changed.**

---

# Change Doesn't Always Have to Be Mental

Not every story needs to end with a big realization.

The change can happen in different ways.

It can be:

* A change in **mentality**
* A new **experience**
* A change in **circumstances**
* A new **relationship**
* A new **skill**
* A discovery
* A realization
* A physical transformation

The important thing is that something is **different at the end than it was at the beginning.**

---

# An Idea Usually Has a Change

An interesting idea usually contains some kind of change.

In a way:

> **The change is the idea.**

For example, imagine a product video.

### Boring Version

> "Wow, this product is so cool."

Then you show a camera that takes instant photos.

There's a product.

There's an action.

But what's the story?

There's no meaningful change.

---

# Example: Rent a Friend in Japan

Suppose your video is about:

> **"Rent a Friend in Japan."**

You could start with:

> "I wanted to find out: if you rent a friend in Japan, how long would it take before that person could actually become your real friend?"

Now there is a question.

And that question creates the possibility of **change**.

Maybe you start thinking:

> "This is just a business."

But by the end, you realize:

> "Maybe friendship doesn't always start naturally. Sometimes it starts because two people simply spend enough time together."

Now there is a transformation.

Your **understanding has changed.**

That's a story.

---

# The Core Idea

Don't think:

> **Story = Intro + Conflict + Resolution**

Think:

> **Story = Change**

The plot is simply what happens.

The story is **what those events mean because something changed between the beginning and the end.**
